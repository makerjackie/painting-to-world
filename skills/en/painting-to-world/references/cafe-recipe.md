# Why the café worked

The quality reference is executable code, not a promotional render. `world.js` builds a street assembled from individually varied cobbles, a continuous building mass, a corrugated/wavy yellow awning, shutters, cornices, gutters, a bell tower, café tables and chairs, cups/saucers, a small imagined interior, painted figures, foliage, and translucent steam. The original deterministic scene reports 2,281 stones and 269,329 triangles. Those are scene facts, not minimum requirements for a different painting.

The orange/yellow terrace occupies one side of a cool blue-green street and draws the eye toward its vanishing point. The useful contrast is a spatial relationship, not an overlay filter. The opening camera is `[8.2,3.25,14.5]` looking toward `[-2.9,4.2,-10.4]`. This camera belongs to the café alone.

Object/world-space paint textures contain broad deposits and fine bristle ridges. There is actual multi-scale geometry beneath them. Transparent steam, quiet leaf movement and sparse motes are secondary. The 28-second tour controls eye and target separately, then hands control back. A sparse landing UI leaves the scene visible. The source includes keyboard/touch control, collision for walls/tables, frame-rate-independent movement, reduced motion, localized help, sound permission handling, and real PNG export.
